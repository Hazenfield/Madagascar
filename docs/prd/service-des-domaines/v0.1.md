# Service des Domaines — foncier — v0.1

**Status:** current

**Procedures covered:** Certificat de Situation Juridique (CSJ) — a read — and
mutation of a parcel from one holder to another — a write.

Main lines only. Each section here gets its own pass later.

## Why this body

Land is where a wrong record costs the most. A CSJ that arrives months late
either stalls a sale or lets it proceed on trust, and land bought on trust is how
disputes are made. A transfer that takes months leaves the register saying
someone owns land they have already sold.

Both procedures live in the same register held by the same administration, which
is why they are one PRD: the write and the read touch the same parcel, and a
design that separated them would have to reunite them at the first concurrency
question.

## What happens today

**CSJ.** You give a parcel reference and you wait months for an answer that
already exists in a register. In the meantime, a sale either stalls or proceeds
on trust.

**Mutation.** A transfer follows a sale, an inheritance, a donation. It runs
through the Domaines, takes a long time, involves several people who each have an
opportunity to slow it down, and finishes with the register being updated. The
delay is not only inconvenient — while the register is stale, it says someone
owns land they have already sold.

## What it should be

**CSJ.** Give the parcel reference, get its situation. Immediately. Verifiable by
whoever receives it without having to trust the person who handed it over.

**Mutation.** A single event, in person. Seller and buyer both present, each
authorising from their own phone, witnesses attending as they already do, and two
registrars co-signing. The transfer completes immediately, leaving nothing
pending in an office afterwards — which is exactly where the delay, and the
leverage that comes with it, lives today.

Consent is the hardest thing to prove about a land sale, and every attempt to
prove it on paper is an attempt to reconstruct after the fact something nobody
watched. In a ceremony it is not reconstructed. It is observed, by people and by
the register at the same moment.

See [the ceremony](../../architecture/apps.md#the-mutation-ceremony).

## Who it serves

- **Anyone about to buy land, or lend against it** — the people for whom a CSJ
  is the difference between a checked purchase and a trusted one.
- **A holder** who needs to prove what they hold, and what is attached to it.
- **The seller and the buyer** in a transfer, each authorising for themselves
  rather than through an office.
- **The registrars of the Conservation Foncière**, who co-sign a transfer and
  answer a CSJ.
- **Anyone who must rely on the register before money moves.** Banks and
  notaries are named in [decisions.md](../../decisions.md) as parties who lose
  money if the register lies.

## Scope

In scope:

- **Titled land** — parcels with a titre foncier held at the Conservation
  Foncière.
- Answering the legal situation of a parcel, as of today or as of a past date.
- Transferring a parcel **by sale**, as a single in-person ceremony.

Out of scope for now:

- **Certificats fonciers** issued by the guichets fonciers communaux. A separate
  land regime; adding a second one before the first works would double the
  difficulty for no gain.
- **Succession.** When a transfer follows a death the previous holder cannot
  attend, so the ceremony does not fit at all. Inheritance needs its own
  procedure and this version covers sale only.
- Every other paper the Domaines issues.

## Requirements

Numbered for citation. An identifier, once used, is never reused and never
renumbered. Each states which procedure it belongs to.

|---|---|

| **DOM-002** | *CSJ* — It is returned immediately, not after months. |

| **DOM-005** | *CSJ* — A CSJ is answerable as of a date, not only as of today. A transaction that relied on one must remain checkable years later. |

| | |
|---|---|

| **DOM-007** | The chain of holders over time is held, not merely the current holder. |

### The mutation ceremony

| | |
|---|---|
| **DOM-012** | *Mutation* — A transfer is a single event, in person, with seller and buyer both present. |
| **DOM-013** | *Mutation* — Seller and buyer each authorise from their own device. |
| **DOM-014** | *Mutation* — Witnesses attend, as they already do. |

## What the system has to hold

- **The parcel** — its title, references and identifying details.
- **The chain of holders** over time, not merely the current one.
- **Encumbrances**, each with when it was inscribed and when it was lifted.
- **The ability to answer as of a date**, not only as of today. A CSJ is a
  point-in-time statement, and a transaction that relied on one must remain
  checkable years later. This means the history is the record and the current
  state is derived from it — never the reverse.

## Success criteria

- A parcel reference typed at a counter returns its holder and its encumbrances
  before the person asking has sat down. — DOM-001, DOM-002
- A CSJ handed to a bank can be checked by that bank against the register
  without contacting the person who handed it over. — DOM-003
- A CSJ re-issued today "as of 12 March 2024" matches the one issued on that
  date. — DOM-005
- A parcel sold three times over twenty years shows all three holders and the
  dates between them, not only the current one. — DOM-007
- A sale attempted on a parcel already under an opposition is refused, and the
  refusal states the opposition. — DOM-017
- A transfer reversed for fraud leaves the fraudulent entry readable, marked
  superseded, with the reason attached. — DOM-018
- When the ceremony ends, nothing about the transfer remains to be done in an
  office. — DOM-016

## Dependencies

- **[Commune — état civil v0.1](../commune/v0.1.md)**, and specifically
  **ETC-007** — a person held as an identity that resolves unambiguously. Both
  procedures here name people, and a parcel belonging to "RAKOTO Jean" is
  unambiguous only once that name resolves to a single person. This is a
  prerequisite, not a parallel track.
- **[Integrity](../../architecture/integrity.md)** — the append-only log, the
  witnesses, and the proof carried by an issued document. DOM-003 rests on it.
- **[Authorization](../../architecture/authorization.md)** — roles and the
  separation of entry from validation. What a mutation requires specifically is
  deferred there and is not settled in this version.
- **[The four applications](../../architecture/apps.md)** — the ceremony in
  DOM-012 to DOM-016 is carried out across `mobile_consumer` and `mobile_admin`.

## The hard parts

### CSJ

- Land is contested. The register must be able to say "this is disputed" rather
  than being forced to pick a side it has no business picking. DOM-010 states the
  constraint; how a dispute is opened, and by whom, is not settled.
- Parcels change shape — divided, merged, re-surveyed. Identity of a *parcel* is
  its own problem, separate from identity of a person.
- Existing records are inconsistent, incomplete and sometimes contradictory.
  Digitizing them faithfully means digitizing the contradictions too, and saying
  so. DOM-011 states that; how a contradiction is presented to someone reading a
  CSJ is not designed.

### Mutation

These are the open questions, not answers:

1. **Authorization** — who is entitled to start a mutation on a given parcel.
2. **Consent** — how the seller's agreement is proven, and how that proof is
   verified rather than asserted. DOM-013 says each party authorises from their
   own device; a device is not a person, and enrolment — how a person is bound
   to a device in the first place — is where the fraud will move to.
3. **Presence is not consent.** Attending proves attendance, not free will.
   Coercion looks identical to agreement from the outside.
4. **Parties without a smartphone.** Common, especially for rural sellers. There
   has to be a fallback, and it must not quietly reopen the hole DOM-013 closes.
5. **Identity of both parties** — depends directly on the état civil work.
6. **Concurrency.** DOM-017 says the register must be able to refuse; which
   conflicts are refusals and which are queued is not settled.
7. **Money** — droits, taxes and fees attached to a transfer, and the fact that
   payment is part of the procedure rather than separate from it.
