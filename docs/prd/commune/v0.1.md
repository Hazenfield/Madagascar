# Commune — état civil — v0.1

**Status:** current

**Procedure covered:** acte de naissance (obtaining a copy).

Main lines only. Each section here gets its own pass later.

## Why this body first

The civil register is the database of Malagasy people. School enrolment, exams,
the CIN, a passport, marriage, inheritance, a land transfer, the electoral roll —
each of them starts by asserting that a particular person exists. That assertion
is only worth anything if it resolves to exactly one authoritative record.

So this is not one document among many. It is the layer everything else stands
on, including both foncier procedures in this project.

## What happens today

A birth is registered at the commune where it happened, in that commune's
register. Copies are issued by that same commune, and only by it.

Consequences:

- If you were born in Toliara and live in Diego, the paper has to make the
  journey. Roughly 2,000 km for a document that is a lookup.
- A copy takes days even when you are standing in the right building.
- Administrations routinely demand a *recent* copy, so the same person requests
  the same unchanged fact over and over.
- Someone whose birth was never registered has no route through this at all,
  only a court procedure.

None of that is caused by the record being hard to find. It is caused by the
register being paper, local, and singular.

## What it should be

Give a name and a date of birth at any commune, or from a phone, and get the
record. Immediately. Wherever you are.

The commune of birth remains the authority that registered the event — that does
not change and should not. What changes is that its register stops being the only
place the fact can be read.

## Who it serves

- **A Malagasy person** needing their own record, or that of a family member they
  are entitled to see.
- **The registrar at the counter**, in any commune — not only the commune of
  birth.
- **The commune of birth**, which keeps its authority over the event it
  registered.
- **Every other administration** that has to assert that a person exists before
  it can act: schools, exams, the CIN, passports, marriage, inheritance, land,
  the electoral roll.

## Scope

In scope:

- Obtaining a copy of an acte de naissance, from any commune or from a phone.
- The birth record, its parents, and the mentions marginales attached to it.
- The provenance of each digitized entry.
- Who may read whose record.

Out of scope for now:

- **Registering a new birth.** This PRD covers obtaining a copy. Entry of a new
  event is a write, and writes are a different category — see the mutation in
  [Service des Domaines v0.1](../service-des-domaines/v0.1.md).
- Every other paper the communes issue.
- The court procedure for someone whose birth was never registered. The system
  has to represent that absence (ETC-013), not resolve it.

## Requirements

Numbered for citation. An identifier, once used, is never reused and never
renumbered.

### Retrieval and issue

| | |
|---|---|
| **ETC-001** | A birth record is retrievable by a person's name and date of birth. |
| **ETC-002** | A copy is obtainable at any commune, or from the person's phone — not only at the commune of birth. |
| **ETC-003** | The record is returned immediately, not after a delay measured in days. |
| **ETC-004** | The commune of birth remains the authority that registered the event. Being readable elsewhere does not move that authority. |
| **ETC-005** | A citizen authorises the issue of a copy from their own device at the counter, rather than by handing papers across it. |
| **ETC-006** | A citizen can see the records the register holds about them, and those they are entitled to see for their family. |

### What a record is

| | |
|---|---|
| **ETC-007** | A person is held as an identity that survives spelling variants and can be referenced by other registers. |
| **ETC-008** | A birth event is held with when and where it happened, who declared it, which registrar recorded it, and in which register at which page. |
| **ETC-009** | The parents are held as recorded. |
| **ETC-010** | A copy carries the mentions marginales attached to the record since it was created — marriage, divorce, death, recognition, adoption, court judgments. An acte de naissance is a record plus everything later attached to it, never a static document. |
| **ETC-011** | Every entry carries its provenance: which paper register it came from, transcribed by whom, and when. |
| **ETC-012** | A correction is a new entry that supersedes an earlier one. Nothing is overwritten. |
| **ETC-013** | A person with no birth record is represented as having none. The system does not invent a record to close the gap. |

### Access and operating conditions

| | |
|---|---|
| **ETC-014** | The register is not open. Who may read whose record, and what they are shown, is restricted. An open register of every citizen's parentage is not an acceptable outcome. |
| **ETC-015** | A commune with intermittent power and no reliable link can still work. Nothing may assume a live connection to a central server. |

## What the system has to hold

At the level of main lines:

- **The person** — an identity that survives spelling variants and can be pointed
  at by other registers.
- **The birth event** — when, where, declared by whom, recorded by which
  registrar, in which register at which page.
- **The parents**, as recorded.
- **Mentions marginales** — marriage, divorce, death, recognition, adoption,
  court judgments. These are annotations added to a birth record years after the
  fact, and they are why a copy is not a static photocopy: an acte de naissance
  is a record plus everything later attached to it. Any design that treats it as
  a fixed document is wrong.
- **Provenance** — which paper register each entry came from, transcribed by
  whom, and when. A digitized record whose origin cannot be traced is not
  evidence.

Corrections are new entries that supersede earlier ones. Nothing is overwritten.

## Success criteria

How I would know each requirement is met, stated so it can be checked rather
than argued about.

- A person born in Toliara and living in Diego obtains a copy in Diego, and no
  document travels. — ETC-002
- The copy is issued while the person is still at the counter. — ETC-003
- A copy issued today and one issued a year ago, of the same record, differ only
  by the mentions marginales added between the two dates. — ETC-010
- A correction made in 2027 leaves the 2026 entry readable and marked
  superseded. — ETC-012
- Asked about a person who was never registered, the system says there is no
  record, rather than returning nothing or inventing one. — ETC-013
- A commune that has been without a network since the morning can still issue a
  copy that afternoon. — ETC-015
- Every digitized entry can be traced back to a named paper register, a
  transcriber and a date. — ETC-011

## Dependencies

- **[Integrity](../../architecture/integrity.md)** — the append-only log, the
  witnesses, and the proof carried by an issued document. Universal rather than
  specific to this body; nothing here re-specifies it.
- **[Authorization](../../architecture/authorization.md)** — roles, the
  separation of entry from validation, and scope by territory. The per-domain
  rules are explicitly deferred there and are not settled in this version.
- **[The four applications](../../architecture/apps.md)** — where ETC-005 and
  ETC-006 are carried out.

Nothing in this PRD depends on Service des Domaines. The reverse is true: the
identity work here is a prerequisite for both foncier procedures.

## The hard parts

1. **Identity resolution.** Matching a person across communes with spelling
   variants, transcription errors, approximate or missing dates of birth, and
   homonyms. Malagasy naming does not give the clean surname/given-name split
   most matching algorithms assume. This is the hardest problem in the whole
   project and everything downstream rests on it.
2. **Retro-digitization.** Decades of handwritten registers, in varying
   condition, held in ~1,500 communes. This is a logistics and transcription
   problem far more than a software one.
3. **Unregistered births.** People with no entry at all. ETC-013 says how their
   absence is represented; it does not say how anyone gets registered afterwards,
   which is a court procedure and out of scope here.
4. **Who may read whose record**, and what is shown to them. ETC-014 states the
   constraint. The rule that satisfies it — who, exactly, may see what — is not
   settled.
5. **Working offline.** Communes with intermittent power and no reliable link.
   ETC-015 states the constraint; how a node reconciles after hours offline is
   not designed yet.

## To confirm

Points where I need to check the texts and current practice rather than assume:

- The exact legal status and required form of a copie intégrale versus an
  extrait, and which administrations accept which.
- Whether anything obliges the issuing commune to be the commune of birth, or
  whether that is only how the paper register makes it work. ETC-002 assumes the
  latter.
- The current state of any existing état civil digitization programme, so this
  builds on what exists instead of ignoring it.
- The legal fee, and what a copy is actually supposed to cost.
